# Cobalt Bank: memo to the board (team-04)

## What we decided
1. **Week 4, everyone ($1.2M):** early pay, free replacement card with payment alerts, instant dispute credit up to $100. Message: the unrecognized card charges are real, what we know and do not know, how to dispute, what the Dec 12-13 upgrade will pause, and that we never text links.
2. **Week 6, everyone ($2.4M):** savings rate +0.5 points for 4 weeks on balances up to $50,000, 150 extra support staff, offline card limit $250 during the upgrade. Message: confirmed cause of the charges (processor fault and fraud tests on cards stolen elsewhere; our systems were not breached), what pauses during the upgrade, the boost.

Total $3.6M of $4M. No statement goes beyond confirmed facts; the migration date is unchanged.

## Why
- The card-charge scare hit trust right before the upgrade. Answering in week 4, the first week allowed, with tools customers can use at once kept early exits lowest in our tests.
- Week 6 lands before the upgrade weekend: extra staff keep support waits short when questions peak; the boost answers rivals' rate ads that week. A boost in week 8 tested clearly worse than week 6.
- A "security" message (confirmed facts, scam warning, what pauses) was the one message style that helped in every repeat of our tests.

## What the simulation says (75 simulated customers, 10 weeks, all announcements)
| Goal (target) | Our plan, 2 runs | No decision, 2 runs |
|---|---|---|
| Active customers (>= 98%) | 98.9 / 94.3 | 96.7 / 94.9 |
| Deposits (>= 97%) | 89.8 / 90.8 | 85.5 / 86.5 |
| App Store rating (>= 4.3) | 4.52 / 4.50 | 4.47 / 4.48 |
| CFPB complaints (<= 240/month) | cannot be measured | cannot be measured |
| Budget (<= $4M) | $3.6M | $0 |

- Our plan keeps about 4-5 points more deposits than doing nothing in both runs; the rating goal holds in all runs.
- With the surprises switched off, the plan loses almost nothing (active 100%, deposits 93.2%): the losses come from the card-charge scare and the government shutdown, spread by customers talking to each other in weeks 7-10.

## What it cannot tell you
- **The levels are too pessimistic to be read as a forecast.** Simulated customers leave far more readily than real ones (10-week losses of 9-21%, against about 1.5% a year at TSB's 2018 migration failure). Read the comparison (plan vs no decision), not the absolute numbers.
- With the faster model the same simulation gave active and deposit levels near target; the two models disagree on levels, agree that the shutdown and word of mouth drive losses.
- Two runs per arm; one run moves results by several points. CFPB filings are too rare in the simulation to measure.
- Simulated customers are built from public statistics and our assumptions, not from Cobalt's customer data.
